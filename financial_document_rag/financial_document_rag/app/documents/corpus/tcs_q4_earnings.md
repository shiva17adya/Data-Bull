# Revenue

TCS reported quarterly revenue of USD 7.42 billion, up 6.1% year-over-year in constant
currency terms. In rupee terms, revenue was INR 61,850 crore, up 7.8% year-over-year.
Growth was led by the BFSI (banking, financial services and insurance) and
manufacturing verticals, which together contributed over half of incremental revenue
growth during the quarter.

# Revenue Growth

Year-over-year growth accelerated modestly from the prior quarter's 5.4% constant
currency growth rate, driven by improved discretionary technology spending among North
American clients. Management noted that the order book (total contract value) for the
quarter was USD 11.5 billion, the highest quarterly bookings figure in the company's
history, providing good revenue visibility for the coming fiscal year.

# Profitability

Operating margin for the quarter was 24.6%, an improvement of 60 basis points
year-over-year, driven by improved employee utilization rates and continued benefits
from the company's ongoing cost optimization program. Net profit for the quarter was
INR 12,430 crore, up 9% year-over-year.

# Operating Margin

Management attributed the operating margin improvement primarily to a reduction in
subcontractor costs as fresh graduate hiring ramped back up, as well as a moderation in
attrition, which declined to 12.3% on a trailing twelve-month basis compared to 14.1% a
year earlier.

# Cash Flow

Operating cash flow for the quarter was INR 14,900 crore, representing 120% of net
profit, which management highlighted as reflecting the strong cash-generative nature of
the company's business model. The company continued its practice of high dividend
payout, distributing the substantial majority of free cash flow to shareholders.

# Business Segments

Revenue by vertical was led by BFSI at 32% of total revenue, followed by
manufacturing at 17%, retail and consumer business at 15%, technology and services at
12%, life sciences and healthcare at 10%, communications and media at 8%, and other
verticals making up the remainder.

# Management Outlook

Management expressed cautious optimism about the demand environment for the coming
fiscal year, noting early signs of recovery in discretionary spending, particularly in
North America, while European client spending remained more subdued. Management
highlighted continued strong demand for artificial intelligence and cloud
transformation-related engagements as a key growth driver going forward.

# Risks

Key risks disclosed included continued macroeconomic uncertainty in key geographies
that could delay discretionary technology spending, currency fluctuation risk given the
significant proportion of revenue earned in foreign currencies, wage inflation risk in
the domestic labor market, and increasing competitive intensity from both global and
domestic IT services peers.

# Guidance

TCS does not provide formal quarterly or annual revenue guidance, but management
indicated an expectation of a gradual improvement in growth rates through the coming
fiscal year, contingent on a continued recovery in discretionary technology spending.
